# Performance and GPU status

The renderer evaluates bounded RK4 Kerr ray integration in a GLSL fragment shader. The inspector reports observed frame rate, frame time, selected step budget, internal render resolution, and actual OpenGL context identity. Ray cost increases with pixel count and integration-step budget; strong-field rays may terminate early at capture/escape limits. The default 48-step budget and 25% render scale reduce work on integrated and lower-power GPUs; the renderer linearly upscales its internal framebuffer to the Swing viewport. The Hamiltonian drift guard runs once every four integration iterations and null-constraint projection once every two iterations to reduce redundant metric evaluations. These controls remain adjustable. CPU particle update cost scales with the selected population (100, 500, or 1,000) and is transferred to the GPU as a small stream of 2D marker coordinates each frame.

The tested development context was Intel UHD Graphics 770 hardware acceleration. No RTX 3050 context has yet been confirmed, and no FPS, frame-time target, VRAM use, or Lenovo LOQ benchmark is claimed. Configure Windows/NVIDIA high-performance preferences and verify the actual context as described in [GPU_TROUBLESHOOTING.md](GPU_TROUBLESHOOTING.md).

The Swing renderer is scheduled at an 8 ms interval instead of a 33 ms fixed delay, removing the previous approximately 29.4 FPS scheduler ceiling. Render scale adapts downward when the measured rate falls below 30 FPS, down to 12%; the slider sets the maximum scale before adaptive reduction. The inspector reports effective resolution. This trades sharpness for responsiveness and is not a guarantee of 30 FPS on every driver. Explicit VRAM budgeting, compute shaders, quality presets, and controlled RTX 3050 benchmarking remain unimplemented. The displayed FPS is measured on the active OpenGL context.
